MNGT 370 — Human Relations & Interpersonal Skills
  • The purposes of this course are twofold: (1) to cultivate a heightened sense of self-awareness regarding students’ skills in managing themselves and others and (2) to cultivate skills in working in team settings. Students will be exposed to the study of human behavior, personality, self-management, self-development, and elementary business psychology as an aid to furthering the student’s success in team environments inside business settings. Students will gain insight into their own human relations and interpersonal skills. The classroom focus is on cultivating students’ understanding of factors that influence outcomes such as leadership, communication, and self-awareness of intrapersonal and interpersonal effectiveness. Specific objectives are:
  • To understand the importance of interpersonal skills in effective leadership, communication, conflict management, career management, and motivating others.
  • To understand the importance of personal strengths and intrapersonal characteristics to effectively manage your time, stress, and career.
MNGT 425 — HR Analytics (9 learning objectives)
  • Students will be able to translate and frame complex insights gathered from workforce data into a compelling story. (storytelling and communication)
  • Students will be able to formulate testable research questions that are both relevant to the organization and can assist with uncovering underlying business challenges, drivers, and trends. (research and discovery)
  • Students will be able to translate and apply HR knowledge to make informed decisions on both current and prospective business challenges. (HR and business acumen)
  • Students will be able to listen and engage with stakeholder requests and recommend strategies and data-driven insights. (consulting)
  • Students will be able to effectively understand systems and effectively maintain various technology platforms such as HRIS, databases, and visualization software. (technical knowledge)
  • Students will be able to extract and work with large datasets, identify patterns, and apply statistical and data management techniques. (data fluency and data analysis)
  • Being able to explain to a non-HR professional how HR delivers profits to the company.
  • Being able to manage an HR budget in a financially sensible manner. This includes (i) explaining the different components of an HR budget, (ii) explaining how investments in the different components deliver profits to the firm, (iii) using financial analysis tools (e.g., net present value, internal rate of return) in Microsoft Excel / Google Sheets to facilitate discussions about delivering returns to the firm, (iv) explaining the shortcomings of financial techniques and how to overcome them.
  • Be able to make the case for adopting or using analytics in HR (e.g., strategic analytics, HR analytics, human capital analytics) to make evidence-based decisions. This includes (i) being able to explain key analytics terms to a lay audience, (ii) clarifying how evidence-based decisions impact the firm, (iii) using credible sources to help business leaders solve a business problem, (iv) drawing a causal model that clarifies how HR activities deliver profits to the firm, (v) reasoning with statistical information to make policy decisions.
MNGT 475 — Compensation Management (6 learning objectives)
  • Understand the strategic importance of compensation to the achievement of organizational goals
  • Identify links between compensation objectives and business strategy
  • Understand the contextual influences on compensation practice
  • Differentiate among various types of compensation tools
  • Build internally and externally consistent pay structures
  • Demonstrate an understanding of the importance of non-monetary rewards in an employee’s compensation package
