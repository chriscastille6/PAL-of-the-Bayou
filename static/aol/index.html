Why this site

The college is building a single place where faculty can see how their teaching connects to program outcomes and labor-market-aligned evidence, without exposing individual students or unsafe small-n cells. Reports refresh each semester; department heads see aggregate patterns and collaboration cues; leadership can trace work to closing the loop and accreditation documentation (e.g. Table 5.1).

Authoritative policies and methodology remain in docs/ in the AOL repository; this folder is a clickable prototype for local launch and committee demos.

Strategic plan alignment (Goal 2)

Quotations below match the Al Danos College of Business Administration Strategic Plan (2026AY–2031AY) (official college document). The same text is archived in AOL_Activities_Strategic_Plan_Alignment.md. The committee agenda cross-references Objective 2.2 (bullet 2), 2.3 (bullet 1), and 2.4 (all).

GOAL 2 (verbatim title):

Implement, Assess, and Enhance Academic Programs and Develop New Initiatives

Objective 2.2 — Assess the Effectiveness of Core and Concentration Enhancements

The committee singles out bullet 2 for AOL’s core assessment cadence. All three bullets are part of the plan:

  1. Develop assessment methods for revised undergraduate courses and new graduate concentrations beginning in 2026, ensuring alignment with learning outcomes and readiness for evaluation.
  2. Assess revised undergraduate courses per Assurance of Learning (AOL) schedule starting in 2026.
  3. Analyze the effectiveness of new graduate concentrations according to the GPIC schedule starting in 2026.

Bullet 2 is the one that names the AOL schedule. The assessment calendar, rubric vehicles, and ETS reporting are how the college actually runs that cadence for the undergraduate core. Bullet 1 is about having methods ready; bullet 3 pushes parallel work for new graduate concentrations through GPIC.

Objective 2.3 — Refine Curriculum Based on Assessment Findings

The committee emphasizes bullet 1 (AOL → UPIC/GPIC). The full objective reads:

  1. Ensure the AOL committee communicates findings to Undergraduate and Graduate Curriculum Committees (UPIC and GPIC) on a regular basis.
  2. Ensure the UPIC and GPIC review findings, identify areas for refinements, and recommend changes to faculty.
  3. Implement the recommended changes and assess outcomes accordingly.

Bullet 1 is the pipe between AOL and the curriculum committees. Bullets 2 and 3 are what happens after: PICs act on the findings, then someone teaches differently or changes a requirement, and evidence is collected again. The governance page is a map of that handoff, not a substitute for minutes.

Objective 2.4 — Identify and Develop New Academic Initiatives to Enhance Programs

The committee references all bullets. Verbatim:

  1. Establish a formal needs assessment process by Fall 2026 to identify emerging academic, industry, and market opportunities.
  2. Conduct annual needs assessments beginning in 2027 to evaluate potential new programs or initiatives.
  3. Recommend and implement new initiatives based on identified needs, ensuring alignment with institutional goals.

This is where structured gap work (labor-market signals, syllabus-to-occupation views, pilot evidence) meets new programs. AOL does not own every step; it can supply repeatable gap narratives and documentation that leadership and PICs use inside a formal needs-assessment process. Closing-the-loop habits (T1–T2–T3, Table 5.1-style rollups) stay relevant because new initiatives still need evidence. See O*NET / NLP brief and leadership preview for how the demo site plays that back.

AI use, limitations, and how to give feedback

Parts of this website were drafted and organized using AI-assisted tools (e.g. coding and writing support). Generative AI can hallucinate—it may produce text or details that sound plausible but are wrong or outdated. Treat narrative on these pages as illustrative until you confirm against official committee documents and data.

Methodology is open for scrutiny. AOL methods and policies are documented in the repository’s docs/ folder and linked from this site so faculty can review, question, and interrogate how evidence and conclusions are framed.

The college does not have full-time staff dedicated solely to running AOL; AI assistance has helped organize materials and build this transparency prototype. If something is incorrect, unclear, or needs to change, please contact the AOL Committee Chair, Dr. Chris Castille.
