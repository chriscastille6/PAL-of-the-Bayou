Purpose

This page summarizes a feasible analytic path for AOL: treat degree-plan and syllabus text like occupational text, extract or score KSAO-relevant signals with NLP or LLM-supported workflows, and benchmark results against O*NET (and chosen focal occupations) as one stream in holistic gap judgment. Empirical foundation: NLP vs. SME KSAO ratings

Putka and colleagues evaluated whether NLP models could estimate job-analysis-style KSAO and interest ratings from job text, compared to human subject-matter expert (SME) ratings. Cross-validated correlations between model-based and SME ratings were substantial across knowledges, skills, abilities, and interests (see chart).

Values shown: Knowledges 0.74, Skills 0.80, Abilities 0.75, Interests 0.84 as reported in the article above; cite the paper directly for committee or external use.

Proposed workflow: syllabi to occupational alignment

By substituting job descriptions with syllabi, learning objectives, and assignment descriptions, similar NLP or LLM-assisted pipelines can produce a curriculum-side profile for comparison to O*NET occupation or task–KSAO profiles. That yields structured gap hypotheses for UPIC/GPIC judgment—not a single automatic “truth.”

1. Curriculum text input HR/Marketing syllabi, learning objectives, assignments
↓
2. NLP / LLM processing Embeddings, classification, or structured extraction; optional network views
↓
3. Extracted KSAO-style profile Weights or coverage scores for taught knowledges and skills
↓
4. AOL output Gap analysis vs. O*NET target occupation(s); feeds governance and closing the loop
