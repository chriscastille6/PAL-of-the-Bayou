Terminology: direct vs. indirect measures

Direct measures
Evidence from scored student work tied to outcomes — in practice, the rubrics (and similar structured assessments) faculty use when rating performance.
Indirect measures
Surveys (e.g. senior, alumni, employer) and other perception tools. They inform improvement but do not replace direct evidence.

Evidence hierarchy (UPIC / GPIC)

Primary: Rubrics — the primary source of evidence: direct assessment of student work on program outcomes (rubric-based course assessments and related direct measures).

Secondary: ETS Major Field Test scores and indirect measures (e.g. surveys). ETS can be linked to O*NET-relevant curriculum signals (syllabus learning objectives → mappings → domains). Indirect measures corroborate gaps; they do not replace standardized direct evidence.

Gaps worth acting upon are prioritized analytic gaps where the college has deployable in-house or network resources; other gaps stay logged for future cycles.

Syllabus-to-O*NET methodology

Course learning objectives are linked to O*NET task statements using semantic similarity (Sentence-BERT embeddings and cosine similarity), following the published Course-Skill Atlas / Syllabus-to-O*NET approach (Javadian Sabet et al., 2024, Scientific Data; DOI). O*NET content is authored by the U.S. Department of Labor (O*NET Resource Center). This repo may also use hybrid or profile-tuned runs; see the methodology doc for disclosure.
