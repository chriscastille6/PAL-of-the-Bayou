Meehl: clinicians vs. statistical prediction

Meehl’s horse race: same (or nearly same) data, two ways to combine it — then tally who predicts better.

When have you trusted a seasoned gut call over a simple score?

Clinical judgment

  • Expert puts cues together informally (interview, chart, “feel”)
  • Hard to specify or reproduce exactly
  • Feels like expertise — especially after years of practice

Statistical / actuarial

  • Explicit formula, table, or algorithm combines the cues
  • 100% reproducible once the rule is fixed
  • No extra “judgment” step at prediction time
References

Meehl, P. E. (1954). Clinical versus statistical prediction: A theoretical analysis and a review of the evidence. University of Minnesota Press.

Box scores across prediction domains

Grove et al. (2000) meta — 136 studies. “Box score” = study-by-study win / tie / loss when clinicians and mechanical rules use the same (or nearly same) data.

Where would you still bet on the expert over the formula?

Formula wins

63 studies · 47%

  • Mechanical / actuarial notably more accurate
  • Typical study modestly favors the formula

Roughly tied

65 studies · ~48%

  • Clinical ≈ mechanical accuracy
  • Parity is common — not a wipeout every time

Clinician wins

8 studies · 6%

  • Clinical notably more accurate — rare
  • No clean domain where experts systematically beat formulas

Domains (Grove criterion types): medical · mental health / clinical–personality · education & training · forensic · financial / job · other. Example predictands: college success, psychiatric diagnosis, job/managerial performance, medical diagnoses. Mechanical ≈ or > clinical across settings (~10% more accurate on average).

References

LO 2.3

Three decision errors managers make

Beliefs about whether a policy or practice helps the business — and the three ways those beliefs go wrong. Next: apply the lens to HPWS/HPWPs.

Which error shows up most in people decisions where you work?

Type I

You think a policy or practice will be helpful for the business when it isn’t.

Type II

You don’t think a policy or practice will be helpful for the business when it is.

Type III · reverse / sign error

You think a policy or practice will be helpful (or harmful) for the business when the opposite is true — direction wrong.

References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.
