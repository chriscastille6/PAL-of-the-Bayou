Does high-performance work practice thinking still apply when you are not a Fortune 500?Day 1 challenge → evidence first, then the talent pipeline tools.

HPWPs in SMEs — meta-analytic answer

Hansen, Usanova, & Geraudel (2025) — Journal of Business Venturing Insights.

  • 115 studies · 124 SME-only samples · global coverage
  • Organizational: + financial performance (r ≈ .13) · + subjective performance (r ≈ .33)
  • + growth (r ≈ .13) · + innovation (r ≈ .39) · − absenteeism (r ≈ −.12)
  • Individual: + engagement/motivation (r ≈ .35) · + job satisfaction (r ≈ .47) · + commitment (r ≈ .32)
References

Hansen, C., Usanova, K., & Geraudel, M. (2025). A meta-analysis on the effects of high-performance work practices in small and medium-sized enterprises: An exploration of organizational- and individual-level outcomes. Journal of Business Venturing Insights, 24, Article e00572. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbvi.2025.e00572

What this means for Bayou operators

Bridge into Module 5: analysis of work → planning → recruiting.

Wrong takeaway

  • HPWPs only work in mega-corps
  • One practice (e.g., a survey) is enough
  • Ignore turnover/absenteeism links

Better takeaway

  • SMEs benefit at firm and employee levels
  • Bundles beat isolated gadgets (AMO still applies)
  • Work design, staffing, and development are the skill side of the system

Job analysis — why it exists

Job analysis is how you identify what is essential about a task vs marginal (often drives reasonable accommodations).

  • Tasks, duties, responsibilities, and KSAOs — what is essential vs marginal
  • Feeds selection, pay, training, succession, accommodations
  • Workflow analysis: see the process backwards at unit and org level
  • Keep descriptions current — dead JDs poison recruiting and legal defense
References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.

Job analysis vs competency modeling

Pop-quiz answer pattern: analysis for current roles; competencies for long-term development.

Job analysis

  • Defines current roles and responsibilities
  • Task/KSAO focused for today’s job
  • Strong for selection specs and job descriptions

Competency models

  • Broader KSA bundles tied to successful performance
  • Useful for development and succession
  • Complements — does not replace — clear role design
References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.

Collecting job analysis data

  • Interview SMEs (clarify why they were selected)
  • Observation — watch the work, not the org chart
  • Surveys — scale when many incumbents
  • Update existing analyses — do not reinvent annually from scratch

Job design, enrichment, flexibility

  • Enrichment ≠ enlargement — challenge/meaning vs more duties
  • Growth-need employees respond more to enrichment
  • Flexible arrangements can improve well-being via control — not automatic productivity magic
  • Evidence first when a partner claims “remote kills output”

Workforce planning — buy, build, or redesign

Cascio & Aguinis framing — planning is a strategy choice, not a spreadsheet ritual.

  • Forecast demand and supply (Markov / transition matrices for scale)
  • Buy when forecasts are noisy, scale for development is weak, or need is short
  • Build when ladders exist and you can develop at scale
  • Redesign work/comp when talent pools are inaccessible
References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.

Recruitment as a supply chain

Informational interviews exchange information; selection interviews evaluate.

  • Generate applicants → maintain interest → hand off to selection
  • Quality and quantity bottlenecks — watch drop-off between stages
  • Diversity: expand sourcing + train recruiters; not illegal quotas
  • Speed and transparency keep candidates from ghosting to competitors

Selection system basics

  • Job analysis → KSAOs → predictors that sample or signal those KSAOs
  • Reliability: consistency; validity: job-relevant inference
  • Utility: validity × selection ratio × SD of performance (callback to Module 3)
  • Preference ≠ validity (Highhouse / Day 1 sales tools)
References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.

Why we are updating Schmidt & Hunter

Teach the revision, not nostalgia for inflated ρs.

  • Schmidt & Hunter (1998) shaped a generation of “big validity table” teaching
  • Sackett et al. (2022): common range-restriction corrections systematically overcorrected
  • Result: many procedures still useful — but mean validities often .10–.20 lower
  • Rank order stays familiar; magnitudes change; structured interviews rise to #1
References

Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter, J. E. (1998). The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology: Practical and theoretical implications of 85 years of research findings. Psychological Bulletin, 124(2), 262–274.

Revised validity ranks

Sackett, Zhang, Berry, & Lievens (2022), Table 3.

  • Top revised: structured interview (.42) · job knowledge (.40) · empirical biodata (.38)
  • Work samples (.33) and cognitive ability (.31) still strong — just lower than .51/.54 lore
  • Unstructured interviews drop hard (.38 → ~.19)
  • Same tools can still be combined; stop treating .50 as the default

Validity–diversity tradeoff (executive view)

Pair ρ with subgroup d — do not optimize validity alone (Sackett et al., 2022).

High mean validity

  • Cognitive ability — strong ρ, larger Black–White d
  • Job knowledge — strong ρ, also group differences in many samples

Often better tradeoff profile

  • Structured interviews — top revised validity, smaller d than GMA
  • Integrity / some noncognitive tools — useful with lower adverse-impact risk
References

Sackett, P. R., Zhang, C., Berry, C. M., & Lievens, F. (2022). Revisiting meta-analytic estimates of validity in personnel selection: Addressing systematic overcorrection for restriction of range. Journal of Applied Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994

Design implications for your selection system

  • Structure interviews (questions, scoring, trained interviewers) — highest revised rank
  • Use work samples / job knowledge when you can sample the work
  • Treat GMA as powerful but not automatic default — watch adverse impact
  • Combine predictors; document job-relatedness; audit drop-off by stage

Needs assessment before content

  • Task: which KSAOs from job analysis are trainable?
  • Person: who needs it — not blanket annual training theater
  • If the job is misdesigned, training will not fix the architecture

Transfer and evaluation

  • Transfer needs supervisor support, practice, and consequences back on the job
  • Kirkpatrick-style levels: reaction ≠ learning ≠ behavior ≠ results
  • Sim Training ROI: (benefits − costs) / costs — watch diminishing returns
  • Optional discussion: “Delusions of Employee Development” — when programs feel good but do not move performance
References

Bauer, T., Erdogan, B., Caughlin, D. E., & Truxillo, D. M. (2025). Human resource management: People, data, and analytics (2nd ed.). SAGE Publications, Inc.

Careers and internal mobility

  • Build pipelines (Markov → leadership pathways)
  • Pair promote-from-within with training and pay (Day 5 bundling evidence)
  • Dead-end jobs show up in transition matrices — redesign or exit paths
  • Retention: development is a signal — but only if opportunities are real
