Mayer / Yahoo — the 2013 RTO memo

Yahoo-style pressure: productivity, creativity, commitment — and a budget add-on. February 2013: Marissa Mayer cancelled work-from-home for ~12K Yahoo employees. We model the assumptions in this note.

What assumptions about work are baked into this note?

Marissa Mayer, CEO of Yahoo

February 2013

Some of the best decisions and insights come from hallway and cafeteria discussions, meeting new people, and impromptu team meetings. Speed and quality are often sacrificed when we work from home. We need to be one Yahoo, and that starts with physically being together.

Being a Yahoo isn’t just about your day-to-day job, it is about the interactions and experiences that are only possible in our offices.

References

Barends, E., & Rousseau, D. M. (2018). Evidence-based management: How to use evidence to make better organizational decisions. Kogan Page.

Consensus vs Gemini

What will you verify before it goes in the memo?

Open Consensus

Gemini (minimum)

  • Allowed baseline for searching and anticipating likely findings
  • Can still hallucinate references — verify every claim
  • Fine for drafting; not optimized for literature review

Consensus (preferred)

  • Better for systematic literature searching
  • Reduces fabricated-reference risk vs general LLMs
  • Deep Search (~$8) optional — never required to pass

Counter-evidence: Grant + Ding on RTO

After you search: a widely shared Adam Grant post + Ding et al. (2024) as later counter-evidence — not a substitute for the memo.

How does later evidence challenge (or support) Mayer’s assumptions?

  • Grant’s claim: cancel workplace flexibility and you risk losing top talent
  • Ding et al. (2024): after RTO mandates, turnover rose ~14%, concentrated among senior, skilled, and female employees; time-to-fill +23%
  • Use this to pressure-test the memo’s assumptions — not to skip them
References

Ding, Y., Jin, Z., Ma, M. (S.), Xing, B. (B.), & Yang, Y. (2024). Return to office mandates and brain drain [Working paper]. SSRN. https://ssrn.com/abstract=5031481

Problem, Options, and Assumptions
Excellent (3 pts)Clearly defines the central problem, outlines options from the case, and identifies at least one critical assumption worth evaluating.
Proficient (2 pts)Defines the problem and some options but lacks clarity on assumptions.
Needs Improvement (1 pt)Problem definition vague; options incomplete; assumptions missing.
No Evidence (0 pts)No evidence submitted for this criterion.
Evidence & Citations
Excellent (3 pts)Integrates high-quality, relevant evidence from the textbook and/or approved resources (e.g., Consensus); cites correctly in APA style.
Proficient (2 pts)Some evidence used but not well integrated; limited sources; minor citation errors.
Needs Improvement (1 pt)Little to no evidence or unverifiable sources; APA errors.
No Evidence (0 pts)No evidence submitted for this criterion.
Recommendation & Rationale
Excellent (3 pts)Provides a clear recommendation to the CEO; explains why it is the best option given evidence and context.
Proficient (2 pts)Recommendation stated but rationale limited or somewhat misaligned.
Needs Improvement (1 pt)Unclear recommendation or rationale is unsupported.
No Evidence (0 pts)No evidence submitted for this criterion.
Professionalism & AI Disclosure
Excellent (3 pts)Memo is professional, one-page, concise; acknowledges AI use transparently; polished APA reference list.
Proficient (2 pts)Generally professional; may exceed length or lack polish; AI use acknowledged but vaguely.
Needs Improvement (1 pt)Informal tone, errors in structure; no AI acknowledgment; missing citations.
No Evidence (0 pts)No evidence submitted for this criterion.
