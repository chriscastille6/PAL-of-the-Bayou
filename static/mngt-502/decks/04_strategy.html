Chasing returns can be counterproductive

Cash-flow analyses rest on assumptions about future returns that are often just guesses (Levenson, 2015).

ROI (short-term)

  • Increased cash NOW
  • Less investment
  • Sacrifice org capability?

Competitive advantage (long-term)

  • Decreased cash now
  • More investment
  • Build org capability — this is your HR budget
References

Levenson, A. (2015). Strategic analytics: Advancing strategy execution and organizational effectiveness. Berrett-Koehler.

Management by objectives (MBO)

Define organizational goals and ensure all parts act in ways aligned with those goals — fit with one another and with the business strategy.

  • Raises questions about alignment, measuring alignment, and monitoring alignment
  • Rebranded as KPIs / OKRs — still goal setting (“old wine, new bottle”)
  • Same problem as Delta: knowing the goal is not the same as investing to meet it

Cascading of objectives

Organization → division → department → individual. Misalignment anywhere breaks the chain.

How effective is MBO?

Rodgers & Hunter (1991): meta-analysis of 70 studies; average productivity gain ~56% when leaders are committed (r ≈ .67).

  • ~45% of variance in org. performance explained by MBO use
  • 68 of 70 studies showed productivity gains
  • Ask: what happened in the two that did not?
References

Rodgers, R., & Hunter, J. E. (1991). Impact of Management by Objectives on organizational productivity. Journal of Applied Psychology, 76(2), 322–336.

The knowing–doing gap

The disconnect between what people or organizations know they should do and what they actually do (Pfeffer & Sutton, 2000).

  • Awareness of best practices ≠ implementation
  • Fear, internal competition, talk over action, culture
  • MBO works — when leaders commit and systems follow through
References

Pfeffer, J., & Sutton, R. I. (2000). The knowing-doing gap: How smart companies turn knowledge into action. Harvard Business School Press.

Year 1 budget & the overage penalty

Stay inside the envelope — overages hit the project grade.

  • Year 1 budget: $1.4M
  • Quarterly budget: $350K

The HR budget has four movable parts

HR Budget = Compensation + Staffing + Training & Development + Administrative costs.

  • Invest where outcomes matter most
  • Generate larger returns on human capital
  • Make decisions that complement each other — not in isolation

Architecting HR

First learn the goals and the budget constraints — then build a strategy optimized for those two features.

  • Foundation first: staffing → wages/benefits → training (sim order)
  • Supply human capital where it creates firm-specific advantage
  • Multiple bundles can work (equifinality) — constraint-aware design wins

Practices that lower turnover

Bundle high wages, promotion from within, benefits, training, participation, grievances, orientation, DEI — message: contributions are valued.

  • Learning/growth + operational practices work together
  • Sim priorities: reduce turnover is weighted — fund the bundle, not a silver bullet

LO M3.1

Practices that lower absenteeism

From Cascio et al. — watch presenteeism trade-offs.

  • Positive-incentive programs · paid time off (PTO)
  • Layoffs · market-competitive wages
  • Training (new hires, promotions, safety, quality) · participation programs
  • Caution: positive incentives can raise presenteeism (working below capacity)

Pizza Shop — cost of capital

$500K loan at 8% → $40K year-1 cost of capital. Profits must clear that hurdle — or the bank takes the shop.

Would your training or pay initiative clear an 8% hurdle if benefits were honest?

  • Cost of capital = hurdle for people investments (pay, training, programs)
  • SGA-style spends should help clear the hurdle, not just look strategic
  • Work the numbers aloud before opening the ROI tool

LO M3.3

Best practice vs. best fit

Best practice (one size)

  • HPWPs help in general (Wright, 2008)
  • Useful if you walk in blind to strategy
  • Risk: copy a bundle that fights your logic

Best fit (“it depends”)

  • Align hire/promotion pay with staffing needs
  • Train new managers enough to perform
  • Apply best practices opportunistically in complementary ways

LO M3.4

Utility analysis — what it is

Dollar value of performance differences from an intervention minus its costs (ΔU = T × N × (Z̄ₓ × r × SDᵧ) − C).

Open UA tool
  • T = tenure · N = number hired/treated · Z̄ₓ = average Z of those selected
  • r = intervention effectiveness · SDᵧ = $ value of +1 SD performance · C = costs
  • Like a cash-flow model — wrong as soon as you finish (Director, 2012)
  • Use to inform assumptions — not to bully with a huge ROI
References

Director, S. W. (2012). Financial analysis for HR managers: Tools for linking HR strategy to business strategy. Pearson.

Cascio, W. F., Boudreau, J. W., & Fink, A. A. (2019). Investing in people: Financial impact of human resource initiatives (3rd ed.). Society for Human Resource Management.

I help by…

I will not…
