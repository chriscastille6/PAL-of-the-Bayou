    Explanations

    How the one-year math works
    Cost → period cost

    Quarterly cost × quarters in place = total cost over the horizon. Example HRIS at 4 quarters:

    $11,000 × 4 = $44,000

    Hurdle → required value

    At a 10% interest / hurdle rate:

    Gross value needed = Cost × (1 + 0.10) = $48,400

    Gross = total benefits before subtracting program cost. Equivalently, net surplus must be at least cost × hurdle.

    Judgment comes next

    The number above is a bar, not proof. Use the program prompts to ask whether benefits can plausibly clear it—without inventing false precision.

    Portfolio note: The sim HR budget is $1.4M. At 10%, the department needs ~$1.54M in gross savings overall. Each program’s minimum contributes to that portfolio target.
    Multi-year check (NPV · IRR)

    The hero answer uses a one-year teaching frame. Use this when cash flows span several years—IRR accounts for the time value of money vs. opportunity cost.

    Cost of capital

    What the company pays to finance operations. If a program returns less than that hurdle, capital would earn more elsewhere.

    Time value of money

    $1 today beats $1 next year. IRR is the rate that makes NPV = 0. Invest if IRR ≥ hurdle (equivalently NPV ≥ 0 at the hurdle).

    Full HR budget break-even

    Sim HR budget $1.4M — minimum gross annual savings to clear the hurdle:

    $1.4M × (1 + 10%) = $1,540,000

    NPV vs. Discount Rate

    Where the line crosses zero = IRR. Invest if IRR is at or above your hurdle.

    1. Why might Finance require a minimum ROI (e.g., 10%) before approving HR program investments? Show hint
      Hint: Finance must ensure capital earns at least its cost. The hurdle rate reflects the firm’s cost of capital (WACC). If an investment returns less, it destroys value.
    2. How does the time value of money affect the decision to invest in a program that pays off over several years? Show hint
      Hint: A dollar today is worth more than a dollar next year. IRR discounts future cash flows—so late savings are worth less in today’s terms.
    3. If your program’s IRR is below the hurdle rate, what options do you have? Show hint
      Hint: Increase benefits, reduce costs, extend the benefit period, or argue strategic/compliance value. For HRIS, strengthen the case that freed capacity goes to high-value strategic work.
    4. How would you communicate the minimum required savings to Finance during an audit? Show hint
      Hint: State cost, hurdle rate, and minimum gross value needed (Cost × (1 + hurdle)). Then link credible mechanisms (for HRIS: transactional → strategic effort shift) to that bar.
