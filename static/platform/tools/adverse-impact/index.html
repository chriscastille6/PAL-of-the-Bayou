Classroom script · firm vs community

How you communicate this

  1. Rates first. “At Level 1, the firm is 12% women; the community is 40%.”
  2. H₀. “If we looked like the community, we’d expect ~200 women of 500 — not 60.”
  3. χ² screen. “GOF rejects ‘firm matches community’ — a statistical flag, not a guilt finding.”
  4. Ask why. “How does hiring work here?” → Sim · L1 hires.
  5. Next moves. Openings from turnover · hiring targets · ER — not training alone.

Do not say: “We’re guilty” or “We’re lawsuit-proof.” Say: composition flag → ask about the process → document. Do not confuse this GOF screen with a 4/5ths test on applicants.

Method notes · GOF vs hiring funnel

Bayes factor hypotheses (hiring funnel — primary for this fixture): H₁ = Adverse Impact (unequal selection rates); H₀ = No Adverse Impact (parity / equal rates). BF₁₀ > 1 favors H₁; BF₁₀ < 1 favors H₀.

Primary (this class · firm vs community): One-way χ² goodness-of-fit. Observed headcount in a focal category vs expected counts if the firm matched community availability % at that level. Composition H₀: firm = community %. Rejecting that H₀ flags underutilization for further review — not a finding of unlawful discrimination. The educational GOF BF is vs community %; it is not identical to selection-rate adverse impact.

Follow-on (hiring funnel): Impact ratio = comparison selection rate ÷ reference selection rate. UGESP 4/5ths rule of thumb flags when IR < 0.80. Chi-square (Yates when any expected cell < 5), Fisher’s exact, two-proportion Z, and an educational Beta–binomial BF (Courey & Oswald communication frame) apply to applicant→hire tables — a different question than firm-vs-community composition.

GOF: χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E · E_focal = N × community% · df = 1 (two categories)
BF₁₀ = [B(α+h₁, α+n₁−h₁) · B(α+h₂, α+n₂−h₂)] / [B(α+h₁+h₂, α+(n₁−h₁)+(n₂−h₂)) · B(α,α)]
