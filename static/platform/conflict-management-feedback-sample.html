Evidence-Based Feedback

Strengths: Creates mutual trust; maintains positive relationships; builds commitments. Meta-analyses show collaborative/problem-solving approaches are preferred in collectivist cultures and linked to better team outcomes (Holt & DeVore, 2005).

Watch out: Time and energy consuming.

Outcomes: Generally associated with higher satisfaction and relationship quality when used appropriately.

When to use: When both the issue and the relationship are important, and you have sufficient time. Collaboration generally yields the best outcomes but is the most difficult to implement (Whetten & Cameron, Ch. 7).

Consider trying: Collaboration only works when both parties engage. When others won't reciprocate, don't over-invest—you can be taken advantage of. When time is tight or the other party won't engage, a quick compromise or even standing firm may be appropriate. Invite when you can: "What would it take for both of us to walk away satisfied?"

Discussion Questions for Your Team

Use these prompts to reflect with teammates on conflict styles and team dynamics.

  • How does your dominant style show up in our team? Can you give a recent example?
  • When have you seen a teammate use a different style effectively? What did you learn?
  • In what situations might your preferred style backfire? When might you try a different approach?
  • If two teammates have very different styles (e.g., competing vs. harmonizing), how can the team work through that?
  • What's one conflict you're currently avoiding or accommodating? What would it take to address it more directly?
