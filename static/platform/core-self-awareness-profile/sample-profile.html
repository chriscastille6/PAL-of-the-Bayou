Cultural Values (Trompenaars)

Cultural values shape how you view rules vs. relationships, time, individual vs. group, and other dimensions. There are no right answers—knowing your preferences helps you work across cultures.

What are these dimensions?

The Trompenaars model describes seven dimensions of culture, based on research across 50+ countries. They capture how people differ on rules vs. relationships, time, individual vs. group, task vs. person, control, emotional expression, and status. There are no right answers—knowing your preferences helps you work across cultures.

Why it matters

Cultural intelligence (CQ) is linked to cross-cultural adjustment, performance, job satisfaction, and well-being among expatriates and managers. Understanding your cultural profile helps you anticipate misunderstandings and adapt your style when working across cultures.

Your profile (7 dimensions)

For each dimension: your choice, what it measures, and exemplar cultures (e.g. USA, Japan, Germany) where that value is common.

  • Universalism vs Particularism

    Your choice: Testify that he was going 45 mph (tell the truth)

    Definition: Whether rules apply equally to everyone (universalism) or relationships and context matter more (particularism).

    You chose to tell the truth (universalist)—rules apply equally; relationships matter less than the rule. Exemplar cultures: USA, UK, Germany, Netherlands, Nordic countries—one rule for all.

  • Time orientation

    Your choice: Future: Long-term goals and possible outcomes

    Definition: Whether you focus more on the past (experience, tradition), the present (current demands), or the future (long-term goals).

    Future-oriented—long-term goals and possible outcomes matter most. Exemplar cultures: USA, Japan, China, Netherlands—planning and long-term strategy.

  • Individualism vs Communitarianism

    Your choice: The team as a whole

    Definition: Whether responsibility and identity sit with the individual or the group (team, community).

    Organisation as system (specific)—task-focused; roles and goals are separate from private life. Exemplar cultures: USA, UK, Germany, Netherlands—clear roles, task-first.

  • Internal vs External control

    Your choice: Effort and drive (internal)

    Definition: Whether you believe outcomes are driven mainly by your own effort (internal) or by luck, breaks, and the environment (external).

    Effort and drive (internal)—you feel in control; success is a matter of taking advantage of opportunities. Exemplar cultures: USA, UK, Nordic countries, Germany—internal locus.

  • Ascription vs Achievement
