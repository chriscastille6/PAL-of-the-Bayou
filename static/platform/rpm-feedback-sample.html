Normative Data

Your percentile score is based on normative data from Matzen et al. (2010), who developed software for systematically generating large numbers of Raven-like matrix problems with normed properties. Their research involved 80 participants who completed 840 matrix problems, with each problem presented to 4 participants across 20 counterbalanced lists.

Education and Cognitive Development

Research demonstrates that education can improve the cognitive abilities measured by intelligence tests like RPM. In a meta-analysis of 142 effect sizes from 42 data sets involving over 600,000 participants, Ritchie and Tucker-Drob (2018) found consistent evidence that each additional year of education is associated with an increase of approximately 1 to 5 IQ points.

These educational effects on intelligence persist across the lifespan and are present across all broad categories of cognitive ability studied. Education appears to be the most consistent, robust, and durable method yet identified for raising intelligence.

Source: Ritchie, S. J., & Tucker-Drob, E. M. (2018). How much does education improve intelligence? A meta-analysis. Psychological Science, 29(8), 1358–1369. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618774253

Fluid Intelligence and Cognitive Ability

The Raven's Progressive Matrices test measures fluid intelligence (Gf) - your ability to solve novel problems, identify patterns, and reason abstractly without relying on previously learned knowledge.

RPM measures a cognitive ability that is strongly associated with what many scholars study colloquially as "IQ" (psychometric g, or general intelligence). The test directly assesses fluid intelligence, which is a core component of psychometric g, making it one of the best measures of general cognitive ability.

This ability is distinct from crystallized intelligence (Gc), which involves knowledge and skills acquired through education and experience. Fluid intelligence is more stable across the lifespan and less influenced by cultural factors (Horn & Cattell, 1966).

Source: Horn, J. L., & Cattell, R. B. (1966). Refinement and test of the theory of fluid and crystallized general intelligences. Journal of Educational Psychology, 57(5), 253–270. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0023816
